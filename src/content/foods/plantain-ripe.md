Ripe plantain has yellow skin turning black, soft flesh and a sweet taste. It is the form fried as dodo in Nigeria, kelewele in Ghana and maduros in the Spanish-speaking Caribbean. Here it is boiled. The African and Caribbean foods chart lists it at a 150g serving, directly below green plantain.

As a plantain ripens, much of the starch in the green fruit is converted to sugars, mainly sucrose, glucose and fructose. The chart shows the result for a boiled serving at each stage, and gives no explanation beyond the figures.

No glycaemic index or glycaemic load is printed for either plantain. The load on this page was back-calculated from the teaspoon figure.

Frying adds oil, and fried ripe plantain is a different preparation from the boiled plantain measured here. The chart has no fried row.

Ripeness is a spectrum rather than two stages, and the chart does not say how ripe its "ripe" plantain was. A plantain with a few black patches and one that is fully black and soft would not be expected to give the same result.

The first row of the pair is on the [green plantain](/foods/plantain-green/) page. Other boiled staples on the same chart include [white yam](/foods/white-yam/) and [cassava](/foods/cassava/).
