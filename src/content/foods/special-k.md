Special K is a Kellogg's cereal of thin toasted flakes, marketed for many years around weight control. Its recipe is not the same everywhere. The UK and US versions have used different grain mixes, and both have changed over the decades. Dr Unwin's chart describes the UK product, so this figure should not be assumed to fit Special K bought in another country.

The chart gives a glycaemic index of 54, the lowest of the seven cereals it lists, and just inside the band conventionally called low, which runs up to 55. The portion is a 30g dry bowl.

GI and the teaspoon figure answer different questions. GI describes how strongly a fixed amount of a food's carbohydrate raises blood glucose. The teaspoon figure also depends on how much carbohydrate a real portion contains. The chart prints the teaspoon figure for 30g and no glycaemic load, so the load here was back-calculated.

Flavoured versions of Special K, with dried fruit, chocolate pieces or clusters, are separate products with different recipes and are not covered by this row. Neither are the brand's bars and shakes.
